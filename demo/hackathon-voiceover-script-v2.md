# Livia hackathon walkthrough — final voiceover script

**Video:** `Livia - Hackathon Walkthrough v2 Highlighted.mp4`

**Duration:** 1 minute 53 seconds

**Delivery:** Calm, conversational and confident. Aim for approximately 135–145
words per minute. Do not rush the figures on the session and physiotherapist
reports.

## 0:00–0:05 — Opening

> Meet Livia — home rehabilitation made visible.

Let the title remain on screen briefly after saying “Livia.”

## 0:05–0:12 — The gap between visits

> Patients exercise between appointments, but physiotherapists rarely see what
> happens during those sessions.

## 0:12–0:20 — Prescribed session

> With Livia, the physiotherapist sets the exercises and targets. The patient
> simply starts the prescribed session.

The cursor first highlights shoulder abduction, then sit-to-stand, and finally
the start button. Do not read every interface label aloud.

## 0:20–0:45 — Shoulder abduction prototype

> First, shoulder abduction. The pose model tracks the body on each frame, while
> Livia's rule-based exercise policy follows the arm from bottom to top and back
> again. It counts each completed repetition and gives short, specific feedback
> when the movement is rushed or the range is incomplete. The model detected all
> nine repetitions in this recording.

## 0:45–1:15 — Sit-to-stand prototype

> The second prototype is sit-to-stand, filmed from the side. Livia automatically
> chooses the most visible body side, then follows the shoulder, hip, knee and
> ankle through seated, rising, standing and lowering states. Six repetitions
> were completed. The first five took 22.2 seconds. This exercise policy is still
> a prototype and has not been clinically validated.

Pause very briefly before “22.2 seconds” so the on-screen timer gets attention.

## 1:15–1:30 — Patient session report

> At the end of the session, the patient sees completed reps, timing and whether
> tracking was interrupted. They can then add pain, exertion and a short note.
> The product is designed to retain useful session measurements instead of raw
> video.

Follow the cursor's reading order: completed reps, timing and tracking, then
patient feedback.

## 1:30–1:45 — Physiotherapist visit report

> The physiotherapist gets a clear pre-visit report: adherence first, then
> movement details that may need review, followed by the patient's own context.
> Livia does not diagnose or change the plan. The physiotherapist remains in
> control.

Keep the pace measured here. This is the most important product screen.

## 1:45–1:53 — Validation and next step

> We have spoken with five physiotherapists who saw value in this visibility.
> Next, we want to test Livia with a clinical design partner.

## Recording and mix notes

- Record in a quiet room with the microphone 15–20 cm from your mouth.
- Speak naturally; do not use a presentation voice or over-emphasise every word.
- Keep background music subtle and instrumental, approximately 18–22 dB below
  the narration.
- Reduce the original exercise-video audio substantially beneath the voiceover.
- Add the supplied captions and check that “22.2 seconds” remains on screen long
  enough to read.
- End cleanly after “clinical design partner”; do not add an unplanned sales
  claim or clinical-accuracy claim.
